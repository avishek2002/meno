# The compounding loop and the defenses with evidence behind them

## How the four failures compound

Each failure mode in this pack is manageable alone. The damage comes from their
interaction, which forms a loop:

1. A hallucination introduces an error into generated material - fluent, confident,
   and unmarked (see this pack's hallucination note).
2. Sycophancy blocks the correction channel: when the learner asks a follow-up that
   embeds the wrong belief, the assistant tends to confirm it, because preference
   training rewards agreement (see the sycophancy note).
3. Cognitive offloading removes the human check: the learner has delegated the
   thinking and, per the Google effect, retains the pointer rather than the content,
   while automation bias inclines them to accept output unvigilantly (see the
   offloading note).
4. Miscalibration hides the damage: the learner's felt sense of understanding stays
   high precisely because the skill needed to spot the gap is the skill that was
   never built (see the Dunning-Kruger note).

The loop's output feeds its input: unverified errors become the learner's premises,
which shape the next round of prompts, which sycophancy again confirms. Nothing in
the loop announces itself; every step feels like progress.

## Defense - citation checking, because fabrication is measured, not hypothetical

[Walters and Wilder](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-023-41032-5), in
Scientific Reports, generated 84 short literature reviews and checked all 636
citations: 55 percent of GPT-3.5's citations and 18 percent of GPT-4's referenced
works that do not exist, and among the real citations, 43 percent (GPT-3.5) and 24
percent (GPT-4) contained substantive errors such as wrong authors or publication
details. Checking a citation therefore has two stages: confirm the work exists at the
cited location, then confirm it supports the claim it is attached to. A real paper
attached to a claim it never makes passes the first check and fails the second.

## Defense - verification must be external to the model

[Huang et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798), in work accepted to the
International Conference on Learning Representations, found that large language
models struggle to correct their own reasoning without external feedback, and
sometimes perform worse after attempting self-correction. Asking the same model
"are you sure?" is not a verification ritual; it re-samples the same distribution
that produced the error. Checks that carry evidence are external: running the code,
fetching the cited source, testing the claim against an independent reference, or
adversarial review by a differently-prompted or different system whose job is to
find faults rather than to please.

## Defense - retrieval practice keeps the human in the loop

[Roediger and Karpicke](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/16507066/), in Psychological
Science, showed that taking memory tests produces substantially better delayed
retention than restudying, even though restudying produced better immediate recall
and higher confidence. That confidence inversion is the point: re-reading
agent-generated material feels like learning while producing the least durable
memory, which is exactly the miscalibration arm of the loop. Regular self-testing
without the material in view both builds retention and generates the outcome data
that calibration practice needs - it is the one defense that attacks the offloading
and miscalibration arms at the same time.
